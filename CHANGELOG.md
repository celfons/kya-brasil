# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Time-stamp on the principal's signature (D-45; SPEC section 23).** The time a self-issued credential records is the signer's clock; an RFC 3161 token gives one the signer cannot choose. New in the core: `timestampRequest(cms)` (the DER request over the CMS's signature value; the caller asks the authority), `addTimestamp(issued, token, { anchors, now })`, `verifyTimestamp`, `checkTimestamp`, `embeddedTimestamp` (token inside the CMS as the signature-time-stamp attribute), types `TimestampPolicy`, `TimestampProof`, error code `TIMESTAMP_INVALID`. `PrincipalProof.timestamp` carries the token, also in `KYA-Principal-Proof`. `verifyPrincipalProof` and `verifySelfIssuedCredential` take `timestamp: { anchors, required }`, both required, no default; they add the rule `timestamp` and return the attested time, which is the one to give `revocationEffect`. In the client, `createKyaAuth({ offline: { timestamp } })`, and the `certificateStatus` callback receives `attested_at`. Without the option nothing changes. Alloy: new module `timestamp.als` (one property holds, one holds by construction, three expected counterexamples) and 120 vectors in `docs/vectors/timestamp-v1.json`, replayed by `timestamp.test.ts`. Tokens were verified against an authority built by the tests, not one accredited by ICP-Brasil. Additive to /v1. Core 0.22.0, client 0.16.0.
- **Vectors from `representative.als`.** Seven run commands in the model and `docs/formal/generate-representative.py` give `docs/vectors/representative-v1.json`: 140 vectors (20 instances per command) of a company mandate signed by one certificate, accepted, refused (signer not listed, another text, no list, seal) or accepted under an assumption the library cannot check (stale list, lent e-CPF). `cosign.test.ts` replays them. No change to the library.
- **ICP-Brasil after Resolução CG 211/2024 (D-43, D-44; SPEC section 22).** The company's signature certificate ends on 2 March 2029; a legal person then holds only an electronic seal, which may not express its will. Three changes, all additive to /v1. (1) **A company mandate signed by a legal representative's e-CPF alone**: `buildMandate({ principal: { kind: "company", cnpj, signed_by: "representative" } })`, the text gains the line `signed-by: legal representative (e-CPF)`, and the credential records `authorized_by.method: "icp-brasil-e-cpf-representative"` with the certificate by hash only (no subject, no CPF). `verifyPrincipalProof` and `verifySelfIssuedCredential` take `representatives(cnpj)` and report the new rule `representative`: with no list, or a signer not in it, the credential is refused, never accepted on the e-CPF alone. `verifyCompanyWill` counts the signer as the first representative. `assembleCredential` takes an optional `representatives` to refuse at issuance. New `REPRESENTATIVE_METHOD`, `REPRESENTATIVE_LINE`, `buildRepresentativeCredential`, type `Representatives`. In the client, `createKyaAuth` passes `offline.companyWill.representatives` to that check. (2) **Seal certificates are refused** as a signature (`CERTIFICATE_INVALID`): new `isElectronicSeal`, `icpCertificateTypes`, `ICP_SEAL_TYPES`, by the policy OID, since the seal keeps the `digitalSignature` key usage. (3) **The CNPJ and the CPF are also read from the DN `serialNumber`** (OID 2.5.4.5), where the resolution puts them; the otherName still comes first. Alloy: new module `representative.als` (one property holds, two hold by construction, four expected counterexamples). Certificate catalogue: 23 constructions. Core 0.21.0, client 0.15.0.

- **The canonical JSON is RFC 8785 (JCS), and says so (D-42).** `canonicalize` already produced the bytes RFC 8785 defines for every valid mandate (members sorted by UTF-16 code units, no whitespace, ECMAScript strings and numbers), so no hash and no signature changes. What changes: the SPEC (section 7) names the RFC instead of describing the rule loosely, so an implementation in another language has one text to follow; the test suite runs the RFC's own samples (sections 3.2.2 to 3.2.4, the sorting sample and the 24 numbers of Appendix B); and what is not JSON data is refused with `INVALID_INPUT` instead of serialized: a number that is not finite (it used to become `null`), a string or member name with a lone surrogate, a `bigint`, and `undefined` or a hole inside an array. Verification paths answer as a mismatch, never with an exception (`verifyAttestation`, `verifyMandateAuthorization`, `verifyPrincipalProof`, `verifyDelegationChain`). New `canonicalEquals(a, b)`. The client no longer keeps its own copy of the function and imports the core's. Core 0.20.1, client 0.14.2.
- **Co-signature by the person responsible named in the e-CNPJ, with no list (SPEC section 21).** `verifyCompanyWill(issued, { anchors, now, acceptCertificateResponsible: true })` counts as a legal representative an e-CPF co-signer whose CPF is the one the e-CNPJ itself names as responsible (otherName 2.16.76.1.3.4, DOC-ICP-04), so a verifier with no source of representatives can still tell the company's will from mere possession of its e-CNPJ. `representatives` becomes optional; at least one source is required, and both count together. Each co-signer check reports `basis` (`representatives` or `ecnpj_responsible`); no CPF in the result. New `OID_ICP_RESPONSIBLE`, `responsibleCpfFromCertificate`, `ecnpjResponsibleCpf`. Alloy (`threats.als`): `ResponsibleCoSignatureSound` and `NoCoSignatureNoResponsibleWill` hold; `ResponsibleCoSignatureLeft` (the responsible left and the e-CNPJ was not revoked) is the expected counterexample. Additive to /v1. Core 0.20.0.

- **Fix: alphanumeric CNPJ (IN RFB 2.229/2024).** Since July 2026 a CNPJ may have letters in its first 12 positions. `isValidCnpj` / `normalizeCnpj` already accepted them, but `cnpjFromCertificate` kept only the digits of the e-CNPJ's otherName (2.16.76.1.3.3), so such a company could not prove its e-CNPJ (`CERTIFICATE_INVALID` or `CNPJ_MISMATCH`). Now the 14 characters are kept, letters upper-cased. Same in `cnpjFromCommonName` and the `cnpj:` line check of `ecnpjSigner`, the legal name read from the certificate's CN, the client's `companyText` (inventory and webhook texts) and its `check()` counterparty normalization (now the same as the core's), and the issuer's /sign page (challenge CNPJ, certificate CN, display). SPEC sections 4 and 5. Tests with the Receita's example 12.ABC.345/01DE-35. Core 0.19.3, client 0.14.1, issuer 0.12.2.
- **Robustness fix: large CRLs were refused.** `verifyCrl` / `checkRevocation` decoded the CRL with @peculiar/x509's `X509Crl`, whose asn1js parser stops at 10 000 ASN.1 nodes, so any CRL above about 1 240 entries was refused with `REVOCATION_UNAVAILABLE` ("Maximum ASN.1 node count exceeded"), e.g. AC SERPRO RFB v5 (~77 000 entries, ~2.3 MB): a verifier failed closed for every e-CNPJ of that AC. Found by the new benchmark (`research/bench`). The CRL is now read by a bounded streaming DER reader (`MAX_CRL_BYTES` 64 MiB, `MAX_CRL_ENTRIES` 2 000 000, exported) that indexes serials by offset and decodes an entry only on lookup; the signature is verified over the TBSCertList bytes with Web Crypto (RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 SHA-1/256/384/512 and ECDSA P-256/384/521; other algorithms are refused), and the inner and outer signature algorithms must match (RFC 5280 5.1.1.2). Same results, error codes and messages otherwise. The real SERPRO RFB v5 CRL now verifies in about 0.1 s; a 1 000-entry CRL went from ~110 ms to ~2.5 ms. Test: a hand-built 200 000-entry CRL. Core 0.19.1.
- **CRLs of the new AC Raiz chains, and a compatibility table.** `verifyCrl` also verifies EdDSA CRLs (RFC 8410, parameters absent): AC Raiz v6 and AC INMETRO sign with Ed448. A CRL that Web Crypto cannot check is refused as unsupported instead of as a signature mismatch: AC Raiz v4 (ECDSA on brainpoolP512r1, explicit curve parameters) and AC Raiz v7 (id-Ed521, 1.3.6.1.4.1.44588.2.1, DOC-ICP-01.01); both remain `REVOCATION_UNAVAILABLE`. A corrupted issuer Name now gives `REVOCATION_UNAVAILABLE` instead of a raw parser error. Tests: the real AC Raiz v3 (ECDSA P-521), v4, v6 and v7 CRLs with their roots (`src/testdata/icp-raiz-crls.json`). New `research/crl-compat`: the current CRL of the 58 sources the kya-icp collector follows, verified with OpenSSL, Go, Python `cryptography`, `@peculiar/x509`, PKI.js, node-forge and the core (`results/RESULTS.md`), weekly on GitHub Actions (`crl-compat.yml`). Ed521: AC Raiz v7's certificate and CRL verify as RFC 8032 does for Ed448 (`SigEd521` prefix, the whole 132-byte SHAKE256 output modulo the order); the only implementation found, `github.com/pedroalbanese/e521` 0.0.35, reduces only its first 66 bytes and refuses them (`go-e521/`). `research/paper/figuras.py`: the paper's figures and numbers from these results. - **Replay protection in Sign in with KYA (opt-in).** `createKyaAuth({ replayCache })` records every accepted request under the SHA-256 of its signed request text until the request's own `KYA-Expires` and refuses it again with `401 KYA_REQUEST_REPLAYED`, in the issuer and the offline modes. `memoryReplayCache({ maxEntries? })` is the bundled single-process store (evicts on expiry, refuses when full of live entries); `ReplayCache.seen(key, expiresAt, now)` is the interface for a shared store; a store error answers `502 KYA_REPLAY_CACHE_UNAVAILABLE`. Recorded only after full verification; requires a finite `maxTtlSeconds`. Without the option nothing changes. SPEC section 14. - **What a revoked certificate does to a mandate is the caller's policy (D-36).** Breaking for core users: `revocationEffect(r, authorizedAt, policy)` requires a `CertificateRevocationPolicy` (`by_reason` per RFC 5280 reason: `"revoke" | "suspend" | "keep"`; `other`: `"revoke" | "suspend"`), validated by `certificateRevocationPolicy`. - **Proof without trusting the issuer (D-34).** SPEC section 19: the agent may present the principal's signed challenge text and detached CMS (`KYA-Principal-Proof` header, base64url JSON `{ text, cms }`, or the request body), and the verifier checks against ICP-Brasil alone: signature and chain, the certificate the credential records, the agent, the CNPJ or person, the issuer, and the mandate hash. The issuer's key then vouches only for the credential's status. The certificate's revocation stays with the verifier (CRL). - **The certificate signs the mandate (D-33).** The challenge text (company and person) gains a `mandate-sha256:` line with the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of the mandate kept with the challenge, so the e-CNPJ / e-CPF signature covers the powers granted, not only the binding. The issuer refuses to issue when the signed text does not carry the hash of the mandate it issues (challenges created before this change are refused; they expire within 15 minutes). Credentials record it in `authorized_by.mandate_sha256` (added to /v1); `verifyMandateAuthorization` checks it offline (`signed`, `mismatch`, `not_covered` for older credentials). The /sign page computes the hash in the browser, warns when it does not match the mandate shown, and offers the signed text and the `.p7s` for the holder to keep; the issuer does not store the CMS (it carries personal data). Test vectors regenerated (new throwaway key).
- **Freshness for any action (D-32).** Actions with no amount (sign, read, call an API) get the risk value the verifier assigns per action name (`action_risk_value`); the amount takes precedence; an action with neither gets the ceiling and the verdict says so. `max_age` receives the action; `checkFreshness` takes `action`; the verdict records `action` and `value_at_risk`. SPEC section 18 states that daily ceilings and their counting are the verifier's. **No business defaults:** every risk parameter of the policy is now required (`require`, `max_age_seconds`, `max_clock_skew_seconds`, new `unvalued_action` and `past_valid_until`), the 90-day and 3600 s caps are gone, and `checkFreshness` / `checkFreshAction` require the clock. `verifyCrl` and `checkRevocation` require `pastNextUpdate: "refuse" | "accept"` (breaking; the issuer passes `"refuse"`, unchanged behaviour). Core 0.13.0: `valueAtRisk`.
- **Status freshness proportional to the amount (D-32).** SPEC section 18: offline, the verifier states the expected loss it accepts per action (`L`) and the revocation rate it assumes (`λ`, per kind: credential, certificate); status evidence may be at most `L / (amount × λ)` old, capped (default one day), with a clock-skew tolerance (default 300 s), or by a function of its own. Fails closed on missing evidence, another currency, an invalid amount or clock, future-dated evidence and a CRL past `nextUpdate`; the freshest statement wins; the verdict records the policy and clock applied and which evidence to refresh. Credential evidence is any issuer-signed answer with `credential_id` and `status`, which the agent can staple to its request. Core 0.12.0: `freshnessPolicy`, `maxStatusAgeSeconds`, `checkFreshness`, `checkFreshAction`, `evidenceFromRevocation`, `evidenceFromSignedStatus`. No network in the core, no change to the issuer.
